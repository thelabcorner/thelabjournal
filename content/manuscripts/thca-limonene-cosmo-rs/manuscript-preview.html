Reassessing THCA-A Solubility in Limonene Through Composition-Dependent COSMO-RS and Solid–Liquid Equilibrium

Solid-state reference sensitivity, finite-composition activity, and crystallization interpretation

Jackson Cummings · Independent process engineer · jackson@thedabcorner.site · 21 October 2026 · v0.1

Abstract

An earlier crystal-to-gas-to-solution free-energy cycle for Δ9\Delta^9Δ9-tetrahydrocannabinolic acid A (THCA-A) in limonene produced mole-fraction estimates of 1.71×10−151.71\times10^{-15}1.71×10−15 to 2.83×10−112.83\times10^{-11}2.83×10−11 at 298.15 K298.15\,\mathrm{K}298.15K. The crystal reference was based on a constrained periodic calculation with an unverified phonon approximation and no converged basis-set study. A separate solid--liquid equilibrium formulation was evaluated using composition-dependent pure-liquid-reference activity coefficients computed by openCOSMO-RS 24a. The new calculation comprises 462 activity evaluations across seven temperatures and 66 compositions, with 41 equilibrium roots independently rechecked against the live solver. Adopting the reported, form-specific thermal parameters Tm=419.15 KT_{\mathrm m}=419.15\,\mathrm{K}Tm​=419.15K and ΔHfus=16.3 kJ mol−1\Delta H_{\mathrm{fus}}=16.3\,\mathrm{kJ\,mol^{-1}}ΔHfus​=16.3kJmol−1 gives a conditional 298.15 K298.15\,\mathrm{K}298.15K root of xTHCA=0.110695x_{\mathrm{THCA}}=0.110695xTHCA​=0.110695, equivalent to 275.42 mg of THCA-A per mL of initially charged limonene. Numerical closure reached 7.19×10−77.19\times10^{-7}7.19×10−7 in maximum absolute log-activity residual. The thermal parameters originate in a patent that also reports degradation before the observed endotherm, and crystal-form equivalence has not been established. The results quantify model sensitivity and provide a reproducible computational baseline for phase-specific measurement.

1. Introduction

Solubility in molecular organic solvents is governed by the free-energy cost of transferring material from a specified solid phase to the solution. Consequently, a liquid-mixture activity calculation and a crystal chemical potential are both needed to determine a saturation concentration. The distinction becomes acute for THCA-A, where available crystalline forms and reported thermal events complicate the assignment of a single solid-state reference. A crystallographic structure of THCA-A was reported by Skell and coworkers [1]. A later patent application reports an orthorhombic THCa structure with closely related lattice parameters, together with differential scanning calorimetry and thermogravimetric measurements [2]. The structural relationship between the two reported materials is assessed from their published unit cells in Section 3.4.

The computational question addressed in this study arose from an earlier prediction of exceptionally low THCA-A solubility in neat limonene. Its values were several orders of magnitude below ordinary preparative concentrations. That result came from a gas-transfer cycle with an approximate low-temperature crystal treatment. Informal process observations of persistent liquid clarity followed by gradual crystallization further motivated an independent calculation and a distinction between equilibrium saturation and delayed nucleation. Those observations were not accompanied by time-resolved concentration assays and are not used as equilibrium data.

This work reconstructs the original model's numerical basis and evaluates an alternative, composition-dependent solid--liquid equilibrium route. The central objective is to determine how strongly the inferred saturation point depends on the solid-phase free energy while verifying the numerical consistency of the new liquid-activity calculation.

3. Computational methods

3.1 Original crystal-to-gas cycle

The earlier cycle used a reported 120 K THCA-A crystal structure, represented by an eight-formula-unit periodic cell. Its heavy-atom coordinates and lattice were held fixed while hydrogen atoms were relaxed at PBE-D3(BJ)/SZV-MOLOPT-SR-GTH. A corresponding gas-phase electronic reference was combined with a 293-conformer harmonic rigid-rotor ensemble, ideal-gas standard-state corrections, and openCOSMO-RS gas-to-liquid transfer free energies. Crystal external vibrations were represented by six equal-frequency Einstein oscillators evaluated at 20 cm−120\,\mathrm{cm^{-1}}20cm−1 and 100 cm−1100\,\mathrm{cm^{-1}}100cm−1. Neither a completed periodic larger-basis convergence calculation nor a fully resolved lattice-phonon calculation was available. The original scripts, checkpoint data and outputs were preserved.

At 298.15 K298.15\,\mathrm{K}298.15K, the saved crystal-to-gas static electronic term was 212.3064 kJ mol−1212.3064\,\mathrm{kJ\,mol^{-1}}212.3064kJmol−1. After all stated cycle terms, the two crystal-to-solution free energies were 79.7767 kJ mol−179.7767\,\mathrm{kJ\,mol^{-1}}79.7767kJmol−1 and 55.7001 kJ mol−155.7001\,\mathrm{kJ\,mol^{-1}}55.7001kJmol−1, respectively. The resulting concentrations were computed using a nominal 1 M liquid standard state. These numbers are treated as outputs of the original approximation, not as experimentally established solubilities.

3.2 Composition-dependent activity calculations

The independent liquid-mixture calculation used the existing openCOSMO-RS 24a-compatible service with legacy ORCA\_COSMO\_TZVPD molecular surface descriptors for one THCA-A conformer and one limonene conformer. Published 24a parameters [3] were applied using the compatible atomic-number segmentation and polarizability mode 0; mode 7 was unavailable for the retained descriptor format. Seven temperatures from 15 \celsius15\,\celsius15\celsius to 75 \celsius75\,\celsius75\celsius were sampled at 66 mole fractions each, giving 462 recomputed activity-coefficient evaluations.

At 298.15 K298.15\,\mathrm{K}298.15K and x=10−6x=10^{-6}x=10−6, the pure-component-reference result was ln⁡γ=0.90897605\ln\gamma=0.90897605lnγ=0.90897605, reproducing the retained activity checkpoint. Across the grid, equilibrium roots of Eq. 2 were first bracketed by f(x)=ln⁡x+ln⁡γ(x,T)+ΔGfus/(RT)f(x)=\ln x+\ln\gamma(x,T)+\Delta G_{\mathrm{fus}}/(RT)f(x)=lnx+lnγ(x,T)+ΔGfus​/(RT) and then refined through 36 iterations of batch bisection. Each iteration requested fresh COSMO-RS activity values at the current brackets. The roots were re-queried independently by a second verification script. No fixed infinite-dilution activity coefficient was substituted for the finite-composition values.

3.3 Thermal scenarios and provenance

A U.S. patent application published in 2026 reports a DSC event at 146 \celsius146\,\celsius146\celsius and an integrated enthalpy of 16.3 kJ mol−116.3\,\mathrm{kJ\,mol^{-1}}16.3kJmol−1 for a described crystalline THCA form [2]. The same source reports a TGA degradation onset near 125 \celsius125\,\celsius125\celsius. The DSC and TGA protocols are not identical, and their reported events require phase and reaction analysis before the endotherm can be assigned an equilibrium fusion enthalpy of chemically intact THCA-A.

The 146 ∘^\circ∘C, 16.3 kJ mol−1^{-1}−1 pair with ΔCp=0\Delta C_p=0ΔCp​=0 is therefore a conditional reference scenario. Additional sensitivity cases changed ΔCp\Delta C_pΔCp​ by ±50 J mol−1K−1\pm50\,\mathrm{J\,mol^{-1}K^{-1}}±50Jmol−1K−1 or ΔHfus\Delta H_{\mathrm{fus}}ΔHfus​ to 30 kJ mol−130\,\mathrm{kJ\,mol^{-1}}30kJmol−1. 4.1 Independent numerical verification

Of 42 temperature–scenario combinations, 41 had unique bracketed roots within the assumed solid domain. The 70 ∘^\circ∘C scenario was correctly excluded at 75 \celsius75\,\celsius75\celsius. Direct re-evaluation at all 41 computed roots gave a maximum absolute residual in ln⁡[xγ]−(−ΔGfus/RT)\ln[x\gamma]-(-\Delta G_{\mathrm{fus}}/RT)ln[xγ]−(−ΔGfus​/RT) of 7.183×10−77.183\times10^{-7}7.183×10−7 and mean absolute residual 1.224×10−71.224\times10^{-7}1.224×10−7. The saved 462-point activity matrix reproduced the prior 25 ∘^\circ∘C dilute-limit result. No decreasing-solute-activity intervals were found over the discrete model grid; the maximum secant Gibbs–Duhem diagnostic discrepancy was 0.00259. These checks establish internal numerical agreement under the chosen COSMO-RS implementation and assumptions.

5. Discussion

The original low-concentration predictions differed from the conditional fusion-based value by approximately 3.9×1093.9\times10^93.9×109 to 6.5×10136.5\times10^{13}6.5×1013 in mole fraction at 25 ∘^\circ∘C. Both methods performed deterministic calculations, but they assigned substantially different free-energy penalties to releasing THCA-A from a crystalline phase. The original cycle's crystal-to-solution free-energy difference of 55.70–79.78 kJ mol−155.70\text{–}79.78\,\mathrm{kJ\,mol^{-1}}55.70–79.78kJmol−1 corresponds to a much larger energetic penalty than the patent-based reference under the phase-matching assumption. Its constrained periodic geometry, small basis, unmeasured lattice vibrations, and differing electronic/thermal calculations provide identifiable sources of uncertainty.

This comparison isolates a modeling dependency rather than independently fixing the true thermodynamic chemical potential of THCA-A crystals. In the present solid--liquid route, the observed DSC endotherm occurs above the same patent's reported degradation onset. The effective fusion parameters may be polymorph-specific, and the crystallographic structure used in the original periodic calculation may correspond to another solid form. Resolving these relations requires thermal analysis paired with chemical and phase characterization.

The available THCA-A crystal structure includes an intermolecular carboxylic-acid hydrogen-bonded ring [1]. Explicit, concentration-dependent carboxylic-acid association in a weakly polar solvent is a chemically plausible mechanism deserving independent study. The mean-field COSMO-RS calculation used one monomeric molecular surface descriptor, and it does not explicitly solve for solution dimer or aggregate populations. Solution association constants, THCA conformer populations, limonene composition, and solvent impurity contributions were not determined in this campaign.

The observation of slowly developing crystals from previously clear solutions introduces a second, kinetic dimension. Even when an equilibrium solid–liquid chemical potential is known, the time to obtain detectable crystallization depends on nucleation barriers, available seed surfaces, impurity content, temperature history, and subsequent growth rates. An equilibrium solubility root is therefore a useful input to crystallization analysis, while induction times and metastable-zone widths require additional kinetic measurements. Rates of THCA-A decarboxylation published at elevated temperatures for extracts [5] cannot be transferred quantitatively to limonene without solvent-specific measurements.

6. Validation requirements

A phase-specific experimental test would first identify the crystalline material by diffraction and chemical assay. Matched samples of assayed THCA-A and characterized limonene should then be studied at controlled temperatures, including at least one condition near 25 \celsius25\,\celsius25\celsius and a relevant incubation temperature. Concentration versus elapsed time can be measured in replicated, excess-solid, seeded, and initially clear preparations while preserving temperature, solvent inventory, and the remaining solid phase. Liquid chromatography should resolve THCA-A from THC and other cannabinoids, especially in heated samples. Agreement between stationary concentrations approached from undersaturation and supersaturation would provide the primary equilibrium check. Independent DSC/TGA with appropriate heating-rate and chemical-conversion controls would constrain the thermal reference used in Eq. 3.

7. Conclusion

The separate solid--liquid equilibrium implementation produced a reproducible 462-point openCOSMO-RS activity grid and 41 independently checked numerical roots. Using a reported form-specific thermal parameter pair, its conditional 25 ∘^\circ∘C result was xTHCA=0.110695x_{\mathrm{THCA}}=0.110695xTHCA​=0.110695 with L0=275.42 mg mL−1L_0=275.42\,\mathrm{mg\,mL^{-1}}L0​=275.42mgmL−1 of initially charged limonene. The pronounced disagreement with the earlier crystal-to-gas cycle demonstrates that the solid-state reference dominates the present computational uncertainty. Experimental identification of the solid phase and equilibration measurements are required to assign a quantitative solubility limit for a particular THCA-A–limonene system.
