## Scientific goal and scope

TheLabJournal manuscript: **Common Misconceptions in Single-Chamber Freeze Drying**. This is a concise applied-science correction of widespread operating assumptions, not a personal research diary or a firmware reverse-engineering report. The key scientific synthesis is the *cumulative and recursive coupling* of sublimation heat supply, product resistance, vapor generation, chamber gas inventory/composition, cold-wall ice deposition, pump throughput, thermal-gauge interpretation and controller behavior.

**Highest-value hypothesis:** during late primary drying, vapor generation may fall below the combined removal by cold-wall deposition and pump exhaust **while ice remains**. Continued pump-down may reduce gas-mediated heat delivery, depress further sublimation, and reinforce the loss of vapor replenishment. Distinguish such a stall from actual primary-drying completion. The mechanism is conditional on geometry and competing contact/radiative heat paths; its frequency or severity in commercial devices is not yet experimentally measured.

A pressure difference at the ice interface is a *thermodynamic potential*, not a mechanical force or observed sublimation flux. Total pressure and water-vapor partial pressure are different quantities. During a fixed-boundary pressure sweep, absolute radiation remains pressure-independent even as its percentage of total heat transfer falls through normalization. The thermal-conductivity vacuum gauge and vacuum insulation exploit the same gas-conduction mechanism of interest here. Avoid invented objections, artificial rebuttal framing and broad unmeasured claims that distort the author's thesis.
