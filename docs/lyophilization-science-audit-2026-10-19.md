## Continued scientific audit: finite vapor-removal capacity

The previous coupled flux relation `j = [P_sub(T_s)-p_w]/[R_p+R_h]` remains correct as a linearization **when the chamber water-vapor partial pressure is supplied as a measured boundary**. It is not a self-contained prediction when the pressure is free to change in response to vapor generation. Pikal, Roy, and Shah (1984), [doi:10.1002/jps.2600730910](https://doi.org/10.1002/jps.2600730910), distinguished dried-product, container, and chamber resistances. A modern process-modeling study, [Vanbillemont et al. (2025)](https://doi.org/10.1186/s41120-025-00115-5), describes pressure-control losses under vapor-throughput and condenser deposition limits. These precedents motivate a deliberately simple sink closure, without implying that it describes all rarefied, choked, or species-selective transport.

For a chamber locally approximated as well mixed and a condenser-pump water-vapor removal rate `n_dot_out = Cw*(p_w-p_b)`, water-molecule conservation demands `sum_i(A_i*j_i/Mw)=Cw*(p_w-p_b)`. The parameter `Cw` is an **effective local conductance** in mol/(s Pa), not a claimed constant physical property across a pressure sweep. The reference `p_b` represents the local linearized removal-path vapor pressure. Each wet shelf follows `j_i=max(0,P_sat(T_s,i)-p_w)/(Rp_i+Rh_i)`, with `Rh_i = DeltaH*Gamma_i*P_sat'(T_s,i)/Keff_i`. Negative flux and ice deposition are excluded, and the model is quasi-steady. It does not determine total chamber pressure, inert-gas composition, condenser capacity curves, or spatially varying partial pressure.

For one wet shelf this reduces algebraically to `j = max(0,P_sat(T_s)-p_b)/(Rp+Rh+Rch)`, where `Rch=Ap/(Mw*Cw)`. For multiple shelves, vapor generated at one location increases the **shared** `p_w`, suppressing the flux at others. This creates a falsifiable well-mixed baseline for comparison with the September distributed spatial model. In particular, the global model cannot attribute residual shelf-specific differences to a local water-vapor pressure gradient, because it has no spatial vapor-pressure field. Its failure against independently measured shelf temperatures and mass losses would motivate a local transport study, not automatically prove a specific flow mechanism.

The chamber conductance also modifies the latent-cooling sensitivity. With locally fixed `Cw` and a wet product, `dTi/dq_in = [DeltaH*P_sat'(Ti)/(Rp+Rch) + dq_loss/dTi]^-1`. This is larger than the fixed-`p_w` value when `Rch > 0`, other parameters held fixed. In a synthetic, deliberately uncalibrated single-zone example with `Rp=1e6 Pa m2 s/kg`, `Ap=0.1 m2`, `Cw=1e-5 mol/(s Pa)`, `Mw=0.01801528 kg/mol`, `DeltaH=2.83e6 J/kg`, `P_sat'=32.8842 Pa/K`, and zero differential sensible-heat loss, a 100 W/m2 absorbed-heat perturbation gives 1.07455 K at imposed chamber partial pressure and 1.67101 K when sink backpressure responds. These are local linear sensitivity values, **not a complete cycle, temperature safety limit, or equipment prediction**. The comparison illustrates why the old latent-clamp calculation can be optimistic about extra heater power.

### Identifiability and experiment

Neither the direct partial-pressure state `p_w` nor `Cw` follows uniquely from a total-pressure sensor and a composition-sensitive thermocouple gauge without independently established response calibrations. A defensible sink estimate requires a measured water-vapor partial pressure, a mass balance over the wet zones or vapor collection, and an independent reference for the receiver/condenser path under the tested operating state. The dryer must also remain inside its controllable vapor-throughput envelope. Compare measured shelf-resolved flux and thermal states against the **well-mixed null model** before assigning their residuals to spatial pressure gradients, thermal asymmetry, or a particular convection law.

The scientific question is not whether higher heat transfer or higher chamber pressure is inherently beneficial. It is which measured resistance governs the feasible vapor-removal rate while the product remains below its allowable temperature. The manuscript's uncalibrated pressure optima and archived spatial percentiles remain conditional sensitivity studies.

## Geometry-dependent gas-transfer coefficients

A separate transport-consistency audit examined the saturating heat-transfer model `K_g(P)=C2*P/(P+C3)`. The free-molecular limit has a pressure-linear slope `beta=dK_g/dP|P=0=C2/C3` when pressures are in consistent SI units. For an ideal planar gap of thickness `L`, its continuum plateau is `C2=k_g/L`. Holding the gas and the two surfaces fixed therefore requires **both** `C2` and `C3` to scale as `1/L`; keeping `C2` fixed while changing `C3` is not a geometrically consistent change of gap. The fixed-coefficient 90/162/408 mTorr sensitivity table remains useful as an algebraic parameter sweep, but must not be construed as one physically scaled family of differing plate separations.

In `scripts/lyo-gas-gap-scaling-audit.mjs`, treating `C2=13.17 W/(m² K)`, `C3=162 mTorr` and `k_g=0.024 W/(m K)` as a deliberately illustrative parallel-plate relation gives an **implied**, not observed, separation of 1.822 mm. The same assumed transport properties across a hypothetical 35 mm parallel-plate path produce `C2=0.6857 W/(m² K)` and `C3=8.435 mTorr`; the free-molecular low-pressure slope remains invariant. The implied effective accommodation at `T_g=260 K` with a nitrogen-like molar mass and heat-capacity ratio is about 0.477, conditional on that single-gas assumption. This calculation is a dimensional and limiting-regime consistency check, not a measurement of the vial or Harvest Right heater-to-tray gap. The two physical paths may have different temperatures, surfaces, flow, gas mixtures, view factors and effective separations.

Ganguly, Nail and Alexeenko (2013), [doi:10.1002/jps.23514](https://doi.org/10.1002/jps.23514), explicitly varied vial separation and measured pressure-regime changes in that apparatus. Additional gap/Knudsen context is available in [Temperature and Heat Transfer Control During Freeze Drying (2022)](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9556401/) and a [packed-bed freeze-drying model with explicit gap-length-dependent coefficients](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6335581/). The revised manuscript no longer identifies the 50 to 120 mTorr vial transition bands as universal and explicitly separates inter-shelf spacing from the actual gas path transferring heat to the product.

## Harvest Right single-chamber refrigerated-wall architecture

The target seven-shelf Harvest Right configuration is one cylindrical drying and ice-collection chamber. Refrigeration cools the cylindrical wall from the exterior, and the product's water vapor deposits as ice on its inner surface. Manufacturer descriptions independently confirm that product moisture accumulates on the inner chamber wall: [Harvest Right, How Freeze Drying Works](https://harvestright.com/pages/how-it-works) and [Harvest Right, Why Is Ice Building Up Inside My Freeze Dryer?](https://harvestright.com/en-eu/blogs/blog/why-is-ice-building-up-inside-my-freeze-dryer). The external vacuum pump separately reduces noncondensable gas inventory; some water-vapor molecules may also leave through it. This architecture must not be drawn or parameterized as a product chamber connected by a vapor duct to a second downstream condenser vessel. Details of the external evaporator copper-coil configuration are based on the target-machine description, not established independently by these general manufacturer sources.

Conserve **gas-phase water** and **deposited wall ice** separately, with the latter remaining in the apparatus until a subsequent defrost/drain operation. At the well-mixed approximation:
`d[p_w V_g/(R T_g)]/dt = sum(A_i j_i/M_w) - n_dot_wall - n_dot_pump,w` and `d n_ice,wall/dt = n_dot_wall`. Thus a reduction in `p_w` can come from gas-to-wall deposition without removal of water from the chamber. Total pressure is `P_total=p_w+p_NCG` for a binary approximation; wall capture lowers the water-vapor contribution, while external pumping is crucial for the noncondensable-gas contribution. A stable total pressure cannot by itself determine vapor production or wall-ice accumulation.

The new `solveInternalColdWallChamber` implements a positive deposition branch `n_dot_wall=C_wall max(p_w-P_sub(T_wall),0)` plus independent pump water-vapor throughput `n_dot_pump,w=C_pump p_w`. It retains the shared chamber vapor-pressure coupling among multiple shelves. When deposition is active, the earlier single-sink notation remains a special linearization with `C_w=C_wall+C_pump` and `p_b=C_wall P_sub(T_wall)/C_w`; this `p_b` is a mathematical reference, **not the vapor pressure of a second chamber**. The earlier `solveWellMixedChamber` stays available as this abstract equivalent sink, preserving the previous regression and analysis. Neither function models frost-thickness-dependent conductance, spatial pressure fields, latent deposition heat returned to the refrigeration system, the vacuum pump's noncondensable-gas performance, resublimation on a warmed wall, or frost capacity. No measured Harvest Right capture coefficient, wall-ice temperature distribution or cycle completion time is claimed.

The numerical tests in `scripts/lyo-internal-cold-wall-audit.mjs` verify an analytical one-shelf wall-only limit, all product water remaining inside the chamber as wall ice when water-vapor pump exhaust is zero, independent fractional pump exhaust, cold-wall temperature sensitivity, non-deposition above the wall-pressure threshold, closure of a chamber with no sinks, and seven-shelf mass conservation. The idealized tests do not independently establish operational rates or manufacturer performance. For future identification, measure pressure with an absolute reference, infer or measure water-vapor partial pressure, record frost-surface temperature and spatial ice loading, and independently account for product water loss and pump vapor throughput.

## Structural identifiability of drying limitations

The single-wet-zone linearized flux is `j=D/(Rp+Rh+Rch)`, where `D=P_sat(T_s)-p_b`. At one operating point with known `D` and `Rh`, the Jacobian of measured flux with respect to unknown `Rp` and `Rch` has **rank one**, because both derivatives equal `-D/(Rp+Rh+Rch)^2`. The drying rate alone determines only their sum. With an independent measurement of vapor-pressure excess `p_w-p_b=Rch*j`, the two-observation Jacobian has determinant `-D²/(Rp+Rh+Rch)^3`, which is nonzero under positive finite drive and resistance. The symbolic audit in `scripts/lyo-identifiability-audit-20261019.py` proves both ranks and constructs alternate resistance pairs that give the same mass flux but different partial pressure. The rank argument assumes a known heat resistance, known reference-side partial pressure, and the locally linear steady state. Practical observability will also depend on sensor calibration and noise.

This is the reason to prioritize independent heat-transfer identification and gas composition/throughput measurements. A stable chamber pressure combined with a repeatable gross drying rate cannot, by itself, establish whether the dried layer, tray, condenser path, or heater controller is the limiting mechanism.
